01 / QUESTION

Can structure substitute for scale in creative generation?

A premise is only a few lines long. A plot must carry motivations, causes, reversals, tone, and emotional movement over a much longer arc. Fluent sentences are not enough; the story has to remain a story.

Frontier models can absorb some of this complexity through scale, but domain-specific preference alignment at that scale is expensive. PlotTwist instead externalizes narrative quality into a modular workflow, allowing a model with no more than 3B active parameters per token to compete with much larger systems.

Working hypothesis

Creative quality can come from better scaffolding, not only a bigger backbone.

02 / METHOD

A reward model, a preference-aligned generator, and an independent jury.

The training signal and the final evaluation are deliberately separated. The generator learns from aspect-aware preferences; a cross-family panel then assesses its output without participating in training.

STEP 01

Learn what good looks like

A Qwen3-32B reward model is trained on aspect ratings created with positive–negative prompting, a strategy designed to counter the positivity bias of LLM evaluators.

STEP 02

Align a sparse generator

Qwen3-30B-A3B activates roughly 3B parameters per token. Direct Preference Optimization steers it using 160 high-confidence preference pairs selected from 5,000 premises.

STEP 03

Evaluate independently

Five open-weight models from different families score each plot using grounded, ten-criterion rubrics. Disagreements can be deliberated and adjudicated by a held-out judge.

03 / RUBRIC

Narrative quality is not a single score.

Each dimension targets a different way a plot can fail. Every dimension is operationalized as ten explicit criteria and scored on a 0–10 scale.

01

Character development

Whether decisions, motivations, relationships, and change form a believable arc.

02

Tone consistency

Whether style and emotional register remain aligned, including through intentional shifts.

03

Pacing

How effectively the narrative distributes setup, escalation, turning points, and resolution.

04

Narrative coherence

Global logic, causal connectivity, world consistency, and the absence of contradictions or plot holes.

05

Emotional turning points

Whether major affective changes are prepared, legible, and consequential for the story.

04 / JURY

The model does not grade its own homework.

Five families. One shared rubric.

The evaluators are disjoint from the Qwen family used by the reward model and generator. Each score must be supported with evidence quoted from the plot, making the judgment inspectable rather than purely impressionistic.

Raw calibration varies across jurors, but their system rankings are highly consistent: mean pairwise Spearman correlation is 0.97 and Kendall’s W is 0.98.

06 / VALIDATION

Before trusting the jury, test its taste.

The evaluation components were challenged with an external contrast: acclaimed screenplays versus critically panned ones. IMDb ratings were used for curation and stratification, never as training targets.

Great screenplays should score like great screenplays.

The test compares 94 eligible plots from the Writers Guild of America’s 101 Greatest Screenplays list with 37 Golden Raspberry Worst Screenplay winners. Balanced subsampling is repeated 1,000 times.

The Cross-Family Jury assigns higher scores to the acclaimed set across every narrative dimension.
